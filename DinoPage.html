Majungasaurus

Majungasaurus was a carnivorous dinosaur that lived solely in Madagascar during the Late Cretaceous about 72 to 66 million years ago. It was a medium sized theropod, measuring approximately 7 metres in length and weighing around 1.5 tonnes while still being the largest predator in its environment.

Majungasaurus had a distinctive wide skull with a short, deep snout which allowed it to hold onto prey which is rare in theropods of the time, and a single horn-like structure on its forehead. Most evidence points towards them hunting medium to large prey including large sauropods.

Fossil evidence shows many occasions of bite marks from Majungasaurus teeth on the bones of other individuals, suggesting that it engaged in cannibalistic behaviour. Giving it the title of the "Cannibal Dinosaur".

T-Rex

Tyrannosaurus rex was a large carnivorous theropod that lived in western North America during the Late Cretaceous, about 80 to 66 million years ago (altho this is an inconsistent figure). It moved on two powerful legs and was one of the biggest land predators known, growing roughly 12 to 13 metres long and weighing around 8 to 10 tonnes.

It had a huge skull with extremely strong jaws, serrated teeth designed to rip apart flesh. These adaptations suggest it hunted large prey, altho it likely scavenged dead animals when the chance arose, similar to many modern predators.

Fossils show that Tyrannosaurus rex had a very well developed sense of smell, forward-facing eyes that allowed good depth perception, and strong hearing. It's been discovered that it likely never roared and produced a deep growl instead.

Spinosaurus

Spinosaurus is a large carnivorous dinosaur which existed in present-day North Africa about 112 to 93 million years ago in the Cretaceous period. Spinosaurus was one of the largest carnivorous dinosaurs in history, measuring about 14 to 15 meters long, but their weights are estimated between 6 to 9 tons and not 20 tons as it was thought for a long time.

The most distinguishing feature of Spinosaurus is the tall sail on its back. Spinosaurus was semi-aquatic as modern studies have shown. The structure of the body is adapted to a semi-aquatic life form in that it has heavy bones, short hind legs and a tail suitable for swimming.

Its head is long and similar to a crocodile with straight and conical teeth perfect for grabbing slippery animals. According to fossil records and stomach contents, Spinosaurus survived mostly on fish.

Velociraptor

Velociraptor was a small, carnivorous dinosaur from the region of present-day Mongolia that lived between 75 million to 71 million years back in time in the Late Cretaceous. This small and swift predatory dinosaur was about 2 meters long and weighed approximately 15 to 20 kilograms.

The dinosaur was slender with long hind limbs as well as an enlarged and sharp claw on its feet, which it would use to grasp its prey. The Velociraptor relied on speed to hunt smaller dinosaurs as well as other animals.

However, like all other predators, the Velociraptor was also a scavenger that fed off any dead animals when it had the chance. Although fossils reveal feathers on the dinosaur, there is no evidence indicating flight or gliding abilities of Velociraptor. Feathers may have provided warmth, courtship display and balance capabilities.

Triceratops

Triceratops was a large herbivorous dinosaur that existed during the Late Cretaceous in North America, dating from approximately 68 million to 66 million years ago. It walked on 4 legs and generally attained a length of 8 to 9 meters, weighing between 6 to 12 tonnes.

The Triceratops is distinctive with its large bony neck plate at the back of its skull and three horns in its face that earned it its name. These attributes might have served as defense mechanisms against predators, or possibly even used for communication and displays. The dinosaur was a herbivore and mostly fed on ground vegetation like ferns and cycads.

The existence of fossils has shown that the dinosaur probably moved in groups or herds at least on occasion, making it less vulnerable to predators including the Tyrannosaurus rex which it was known to be at odds with.

Giganotosaurus

The Giganotosaurus was a large carnivorous theropod that roamed in the area that is now part of Argentina during the Late Cretaceous period between 99 and 95 million years ago. The Giganotosaurus was one of the biggest land predators ever, reaching lengths of around 12 to 13 meters and weighing 6 to 8 tons.

It possessed a very large head with serrated teeth designed for making large slice wounds on prey. The Giganotosaurus was much lighter in structure and had much less power in its bite compared to other predators like the Tyrannosaurus rex which while similar in appearance it was not closely related to.

According to paleontologists, its hunting methods depended more on strong neck muscles and slash-and-tear actions to kill giant herbivores like Argentinosaurus. There are some suggestions that the Giganotosaurus may have hunted in packs but this is not widely agreed on or accepted.

Chicken

Chickens are domesticated birds which evolved from wild jungle-fowl found in Southeast Asia. Even though they do not resemble dinosaurs at first sight, chickens are classified as avian dinosaurs and they are descendants of extinct theropods such as Velociraptor and T. Rex.

Just like their predecessors, chickens walk upright, reproduce by laying eggs, and have hollow bones. Scientific research based on fossil records and genetic analysis has proven that birds are direct descendants of small feathered theropod dinosaurs.

In other words, dinosaurs did not really become extinct; only non-avian dinosaurs became extinct because of the mass extinction event about 66 million years ago, while avian dinosaurs survived and kept evolving into current bird species including chickens.

Ankylosaurus

Ankylosaurus was an armored herbivore that lived in North America during the late Cretaceous, around 68 million to 66 million years ago. Ankylosaurus was a quadrupedal dinosaur that could reach lengths up to 8 meters and could weigh between 4 to 8 tons.

Ankylosaurus was covered by armor, with plates covering its whole body and culminating in a club-tipped tail. The club-tailed tail of Ankylosaurus was used as a form of defense from other larger dinosaurs, and it is also suspected that this tail caused a T-Rex’s death based on fossils found.

Ankylosaurus had a large bulky body and small legs that allowed it to remain closer to the ground while walking, this gave it yet another advantage against larger predatory dinosaurs.

Brachiosaurus

Brachiosaurus was an immense herbivorous dinosaur that lived during the Late Jurassic period, about 154 to 150 million years ago, in what is now North America. Brachiosaurus was a huge sauropod that measured between 20 to 26 meters in length and weighed as much as 50 tonnes but possibly more.

Unusually for sauropods, Brachiosaurus had longer forelimbs than hind-limbs, which created an impressive physique with a vertically inclined profile. The long neck of Brachiosaurus made it possible to browse at greater heights than most other herbivores could reach.

Brachiosaurus mostly fed on tree canopies and tall vegetation. Although it was such an immense beast, it is believed to have been relatively passive and dependent on its intimidating size for defense against predators.
